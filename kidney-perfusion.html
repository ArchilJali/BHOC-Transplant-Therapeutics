Kidney perfusion and oxygen delivery for transplantation

Kidney perfusion research connects oxygen delivery, metabolism and renal function. Explore the methods, perfusates and measurements used from the organ circuit to transplantation.

What is kidney perfusion?

Kidney perfusion moves fluid through the organ's blood vessels. Before transplantation, an ex vivo circuit gives researchers access to the kidney's flow, pressure and metabolism. The perfusate, temperature and oxygen supply define the experiment.

Hypothermic machine perfusion keeps the kidney cold while circulating preservation fluid. Normothermic machine perfusion works near body temperature and supports measurements of active metabolism. Static cold storage uses a preservation solution without ongoing circulation. These methods address different practical and biological questions.

Name the organ model and the next validation step

An ex vivo kidney experiment can assess oxygen consumption, vascular response and measured function during perfusion. A transplant model adds a different question: how the organ performs after it returns to a recipient.

The BHOC programme starts with characterization and controlled organ-specific testing. Findings from a historical carrier or a red-cell experiment can inform that work, while BHOC-specific performance needs its own evidence.

A rat kidney-preservation study at 10°C

The historical catalogue includes kidney preservation at 10°C with Hemopure and sodium thiosulfate in a rat syngeneic orthotopic transplantation model. The named interventions and the animal model are essential parts of the record.

This study is not a human BHOC trial. The existing evidence brief links the original publication and separates the experimental finding from the BHOC development question.

Functional oxygen handling in isolated human kidneys

The RBC-storage analysis describes isolated human kidneys perfused ex vivo with differently stored donor red-cell preparations. It examines organ oxygen handling rather than a BHOC intervention.

Its respiratory-rate and oxygen-diffusion-capacity findings are separate measures. They should not be collapsed into one general improvement percentage or described as patient-transfusion or post-transplant outcomes. The source is useful for designing functional readouts.

From perfusion measurements to graft function

Moers and colleagues compared hypothermic machine perfusion with cold storage in a paired-kidney randomized trial involving 336 deceased donors. The primary outcome was delayed graft function, defined in that trial as dialysis during the first week after transplantation. That recipient outcome differs from a measurement made while the kidney is on a circuit.

Original study: Moers and colleagues

Oxygen delivery in a working perfusate

Aburawi and colleagues perfused 14 discarded human kidneys for six hours at a mean temperature of 37°C. Seven received Hemopure (HBOC-201), a historical hemoglobin-based carrier and seven received packed red cells. The team measured vascular resistance, oxygen extraction, metabolic activity, energy stores and tissue structure.

This is useful for designing an oxygen-delivery experiment: define the carrier, then measure what reaches the organ and what the organ uses. Flow, oxygen consumption and ATP describe different parts of that process. Creatinine clearance and urine measurements add information about kidney function.

Define the perfusate and the readouts together

A BHOC kidney study would characterize the formulation and oxygen-affinity profile under the proposed temperature, flow and gas conditions. Carrier concentration, oxygen content, extraction, methemoglobin, vascular resistance and organ-specific function should be interpreted together.

A useful collaboration identifies which question the model can answer and which subsequent validation step remains. Ex vivo function, animal autotransplantation and clinical transplantation are not interchangeable endpoints.

Questions for BHOC kidney research

Does a cell-free perfusate contain an oxygen carrier? The term describes the absence of intact cells. The formulation must name the carrier, its concentration and the conditions in which it is tested.

What do we want to measure? BHOC Transplant focuses on sustained oxygen delivery adapted to tissue demand. A kidney programme would connect formulation and oxygen-unloading measurements with vascular response, metabolism and renal function. Historical carrier studies help frame those experiments.
