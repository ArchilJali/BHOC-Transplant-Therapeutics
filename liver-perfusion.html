Liver perfusion, temperature and oxygen

Liver machine perfusion circulates fluid through a donor liver outside the body. Normothermic machine perfusion (NMP) runs near body temperature. Hypothermic oxygenated perfusion (HOPE) supplies oxygenated fluid under cold conditions.

A useful comparison specifies the temperature, flow, perfusate and duration. These conditions shape how oxygen delivery and liver function are measured.

A cell-free oxygen carrier in a human liver model

Laing and colleagues studied five discarded high-risk human livers with a Hemopure-based perfusate and matched them to five livers perfused with red cells. Over six hours of normothermic perfusion, the team compared oxygen extraction, metabolic activity, flow and tissue structure.

In this experiment, livers perfused with Hemopure (HBOC-201) extracted more oxygen than those perfused with red cells. The study ended with the isolated liver and did not follow recipients.

Assessing liver cells and bile ducts

Matton and colleagues studied bile chemistry during six hours of end-ischemic NMP in 23 human donor livers. Higher bile pH and bicarbonate and lower bile glucose were associated with less injury on bile-duct histology. A separate six-liver clinical pilot included these measurements in its selection criteria.

Lactate handling and bile chemistry address different aspects of liver function. Read each measurement alongside the perfusion protocol, sampling conditions and tissue findings.

Matton et al. on bile chemistry and bile-duct injury (PMID 30395120)

Connecting organ measurements with transplant outcomes

In the DHOPE-DCD trial, 156 patients received livers from donors after circulatory death. Nonanastomotic biliary strictures within six months occurred in 6% of recipients after hypothermic oxygenated perfusion and 18% after static cold storage alone.

An isolated-liver measurement and a recipient outcome answer different questions. Within Precision Oxygen Therapeutics, BHOC Transplant aims to match oxygen delivery to tissue demand. Liver studies would measure oxygen unloading, vascular response and organ function under a defined protocol before studying transplant outcomes.

The DHOPE-DCD randomized trial (PMID 33626248)

Circuit reliability, metabolism and organ function

The experimental-liver brief compares two methods papers. Nassar’s pig-liver work included a transplant simulation rather than recipient-animal transplantation. Beal’s rat-liver paper examined ex vivo methods and did not include subsequent transplantation.

Neither tested BHOC. They are useful because a perfusate programme must distinguish reliable circuit operation, biochemical injury, energy metabolism and measured organ function. These endpoints should not be treated as interchangeable.

Temperature defines the experimental conditions

Normothermic, hypothermic and cold-to-warm protocols create different operating conditions. The existing Science page treats temperature as a variable to characterize, not an assumption that a carrier has one fixed oxygen-release behaviour across the transplant journey.

Historical HBOC-201 P50 near 40 mmHg is described at 37°C in the scientific material. It is not a constant from 4 to 37°C, and a new BHOC formulation requires its own measurements.

A defined liver-perfusate research question

A liver study should connect the input perfusate to oxygen delivery and consumption, ATP, injury markers, vascular behaviour and organ-specific function under controlled conditions. A comparison should make its carrier, temperature and flow choices explicit.

BHOC Transplant proposes controlled ex vivo work as an early development stage. Further transplantation and clinical questions require separate protocols and their own evidence.
