What organ sustainability means at BHOC Transplant

Organ sustainability is our research term for following a donor organ's function across recovery, transport, rewarming and transplantation. The question changes at each stage. Before implantation, can the team assess function under defined conditions? After implantation, how well does the organ work in the recipient, and for how long?

The transplant literature uses organ preservation to describe methods such as static cold storage and machine perfusion. Those methods can be evaluated against several outcomes. How many organs are used, how much tissue injury is measured and how recipients recover each require their own data.

Count the organs that reach transplantation

In a randomized study that included 220 liver transplants, Nasralla and colleagues compared normothermic machine perfusion with static cold storage. The normothermic group had about half the discard rate and about half the graft injury measured by liver enzyme release. The trial reported no statistically significant difference in bile duct complications, graft survival or patient survival.

Discard describes the pathway from organs considered for transplantation to organs actually used. Enzyme release describes injury in the organs studied. Survival follows the recipient or graft over time. Report the denominator and the follow-up period beside each result.

Nasralla et al. on normothermic liver perfusion

Follow the outcome that matters for the organ

Van Rijn and colleagues examined a different question in liver transplantation from donors after circulatory death. In their randomized trial, 78 recipients received livers treated with hypothermic oxygenated machine perfusion and 78 received livers after static cold storage. The primary outcome was nonanastomotic biliary strictures within six months. The study reported strictures in 6% and 18% of recipients, respectively.

The two studies used different perfusion conditions and endpoints. Their results show why the protocol, organ selection and follow-up belong beside every outcome. A count of organs transplanted cannot describe how their bile ducts function months later.

Van Rijn et al. on hypothermic oxygenated liver perfusion

Connect oxygen measurements with later function

BHOC Transplant studies questions within Precision Oxygen Therapeutics. For a proposed Biological Hemoglobin Oxygen Carrier study, record the perfusion conditions and oxygen delivery at the stage where they are measured. Specify the organ function or injury measures and the relevant comparator before comparing results.

A study that follows the organ into the recipient can relate perfusion measurements to subsequent graft function. Organ sustainability gives that research a practical structure. Track which organs were assessed, what could be measured during support and what happened after transplantation.
