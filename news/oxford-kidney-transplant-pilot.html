Oxford pilot gives declined donor kidneys a second assessment

Oxford University Hospitals has launched a pilot using machine perfusion with oxygenated blood to reassess donor kidneys declined at standard assessment.

BHOC Transplant · 10 November 2026

Oxford University Hospitals announced the launch of its kidney transplant pilot on 9 November 2026. The Oxford Assessment and Recovery Centre at Churchill Hospital is part of an NHS Blood and Transplant programme. It can take referrals for donated kidneys that were not accepted during standard assessment.

What the pilot changes

A machine circulates oxygenated blood through the kidney outside the body. This gives clinicians time to assess how the kidney is functioning and, where appropriate, to take a biopsy before deciding whether it is suitable for transplantation.

The programme separates the donation, specialist assessment and transplant stages. In this model, an organ may be donated at one hospital, reassessed at a second and transplanted at a third.

What to follow

For organ sustainability, a useful question is which measurements during perfusion help predict how a kidney will function after transplant. The launch announcement describes the assessment process. It does not report how many kidneys the Oxford centre has transplanted through the pilot or how those kidneys subsequently performed.

The Oxford announcement describes perfusion with oxygenated blood. It does not report an evaluation of BHOC.
